OPT Unemployment Days Explained: The 90-Day and 150-Day Rule

September 24, 2026 · 6 min read · Growx Tech IT

F-1 students on OPT get 90 days of allowed unemployment; with the STEM extension, that becomes 150 cumulative days. Here's exactly how the clock works, and what to do if you're getting close.

The short answer: you're allowed a maximum of 90 cumulative days of unemployment during your standard 12-month post-completion OPT. If you later get the 24-month STEM OPT extension, you get 60 more days on top of that, for a combined cumulative limit of 150 days across the whole OPT-plus-STEM-OPT period, not a fresh 90 followed by a fresh 150.

That's the rule in one paragraph. Everything else is detail that trips people up, so here's the detail.

The clock starts on your I-20 date, not your graduation date

A lot of students assume the countdown begins when they start job hunting, or when their program officially ends. It doesn't. The unemployment clock starts on the OPT start date printed on your Form I-20, the date USCIS approved your OPT to begin. If there's a gap between your program end date and your OPT start date, that gap doesn't count against you, but every day from your official OPT start date onward does, unless you're in qualifying paid employment related to your field of study.

What counts as "unemployment" on the clock

It's broader than just "no job at all." Days count against your limit if you are:

  • Between jobs, with no qualifying employment
  • Working, but not in a role related to your field of study
  • On STEM OPT and working fewer than 20 hours per week for your reporting employer
  • Working unpaid, in most cases (paid employment is generally required to qualify)

Time spent actively applying, interviewing, or waiting on an offer still counts as unemployment days on the clock. The system doesn't distinguish between "not trying" and "trying hard but nothing yet."

90 days on initial OPT, 150 total with the STEM extension

According to DHS's Study in the States STEM OPT Extension Overview, the standard post-completion OPT period carries a 90-day maximum period of unemployment. Students who qualify for and receive the 24-month STEM OPT extension are granted an additional 60 days, bringing the cumulative total to 150 days across both periods combined.

To qualify for the STEM OPT extension itself, you generally need: a degree in an eligible STEM field from a SEVP-certified school, an employer enrolled in E-Verify, a formal I-983 training plan with the employer, and at least 20 hours of work per week for that employer.

Exceeding your cumulative limit, 90 days on initial OPT or 150 with the STEM extension, means you're considered to have violated F-1 status. That can complicate a future H-1B change of status or other immigration filings. If you're within a few weeks of your limit, contact your school's Designated School Official (DSO) immediately; they're the only ones who can officially confirm your status.

DHS Study in the States, STEM OPT Extension Overview

Does the H-1B cap-gap extension change the day count?

No, and this is a common point of confusion. The cap-gap extension keeps your F-1 status and work authorization active between your OPT or STEM OPT end date and your H-1B start date, but only if your H-1B petition was timely filed and requests a change of status. It's a separate mechanism from the unemployment clock: it doesn't add days to your 90 or 150-day allowance, and it doesn't reset anything. It simply bridges the gap in status so you're not left without authorization while your H-1B is pending.
