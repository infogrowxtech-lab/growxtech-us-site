How to Get an IT Job in Austin, Texas in 2026

September 22, 2026 · 7 min read · Growx Tech IT

Austin just landed at No. 5 among North American tech talent markets, with the highest concentration of software engineers of any major metro. Here's what the data actually shows, and how to position yourself for a role there.

Austin's reputation as a tech hub isn't just marketing copy. New 2026 data on both the talent side and the local economy backs it up, and the shape of that data has real implications if you're actually job hunting there right now rather than just reading headlines about it.

Austin is now a top-5 North American tech market

CBRE's "Scoring Tech Talent 2026" report, an annual ranking of North American metros by the size, growth, and concentration of their tech workforce, placed Austin at No. 5 out of the markets it evaluated, behind only the San Francisco Bay Area, Seattle, Toronto, and the New York metro. What stands out about Austin's placement specifically is workforce composition: 63% of Austin's tech workforce works as software engineers, the highest concentration of any top market in the report, and roughly 17% of the city's tech workers hold AI-specialty skills, putting Austin among the more AI-concentrated markets nationally as well.

The local economy backs up the ranking

Talent rankings can feel abstract, but Austin's broader labor-market data lines up with them. According to Opportunity Austin's economic indicators, sourced from the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the Austin metro's unemployment rate stood at 4.0% in July 2026, lower than both the Texas rate (4.8%) and the national rate (4.4%). The region added 23,800 jobs between July 2025 and July 2026, a 1.7% growth rate that was the second-fastest among the 50 largest U.S. metros over that period. Professional and business services, the category that includes much of the region's IT, consulting, and tech-adjacent hiring, added 9,200 jobs on its own, a 3.2% growth rate. On the investment side, Austin-area venture capital funding reached $6.5 billion year-to-date through 2026, more than double the $2.9 billion raised over the same period in 2025, and local inventors filed 2,896 patents year-to-date, up 14% from a year earlier.

Myth worth retiring: "Austin tech is just software engineers." The 63% software-engineer concentration is real, but it describes the existing workforce, not the only door in. Professional and business services, the broader category covering IT support, business analysis, project coordination, and consulting-adjacent roles, grew a solid 3.2% over the same period, and a metro pulling in $6.5 billion in fresh VC funding needs plenty of people who aren't writing code full time: operations, customer success, recruiting, and implementation roles all scale alongside engineering headcount at growing companies.

CBRE, "Scoring Tech Talent 2026"; Opportunity Austin, Monthly Economic Indicators, September 2026

What this means if you're job hunting in Austin right now

A market this concentrated in software engineering means direct competition for engineering titles is genuinely stiff, both from the volume of engineers already there and from candidates relocating to a market with this much momentum. If your background is closer to IT support, business analysis, QA, project coordination, or another tech-adjacent role, the growth in professional and business services is a more realistic entry point, and it's worth framing your resume around that category specifically rather than competing head-on for engineer titles you may not have direct experience for. The scale of new VC funding flowing into the region also matters for how you approach the search itself: a market with this much fresh startup capital has a higher share of smaller, growth-stage employers than a legacy enterprise market, which usually means faster-moving, less formal hiring processes and more weight placed on a candidate's specific, recent project experience over pedigree alone.

A mix of large employers and growth-stage startups

Austin's tech base isn't built around one dominant employer the way some smaller metros are. The market is anchored by a long-established base of large technology and semiconductor employers with a major Austin presence, alongside a fast-growing layer of venture-backed startups fueled by the $6.5 billion in 2026 funding noted above. For job seekers, that mix matters practically: a large, established employer typically means a more structured, multi-stage interview process and a slower timeline, while a growth-stage startup usually means fewer interview rounds, faster decisions, and more emphasis on what you can demonstrably do in the first 90 days than on where you've worked before. Knowing which type of company you're applying to, and adjusting your resume framing and interview prep accordingly, matters more in a market with this much of both.

How Austin compares to Texas's other fast-growing metro

Austin isn't the only Texas market showing up near the top of national tech rankings this year. Dallas-Fort Worth placed No. 8 in the same CBRE report, with a different growth story built more around AI-adjacent hiring inside financial services, healthcare, and enterprise-technology employers than around a pure software-engineering concentration. If Austin's engineer-heavy market feels like a tighter fit for a non-engineering background, it's worth knowing the two metros are pulling on different levers, and worth reading our DFW breakdown if that market fits your background better.

Practical steps for landing a role in this market

A few things matter more in a market moving this fast: applying quickly once a role posts, since high-growth markets tend to fill roles faster than average; tailoring your resume summary to name the specific category you're targeting (software engineering, IT support, business analysis, or implementation) rather than a generic "IT professional" framing that doesn't stand out in a market this concentrated; researching whether a given opening is at an established employer or a growth-stage startup before your interview, so your prep matches the pace and format you'll actually get; and being ready to speak concretely about any AI-adjacent tools you've used, even informally, given how large a share of the local growth and investment is tied to AI-specialty hiring specifically.

The bottom line

Austin's tech market growth in 2026 is backed by real labor-market and investment data, not just reputation, but it's a market with real competition, especially for engineering roles, and it rewards knowing which part of the market you're actually applying into.
