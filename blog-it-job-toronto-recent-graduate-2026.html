How to Get an IT Job in Toronto as a Recent Grad in 2026

November 23, 2026 · 7 min read · Growx Tech IT

Toronto ranks No. 3 in North America for tech talent, per CBRE's 2026 report, and has added more tech jobs since 2022 than any other market it tracks. Here's what that growth actually looks like, and how to position yourself for it as a recent grad.

Toronto shows up near the top of almost every recent ranking of North America's best tech markets, and that's not just marketing — the underlying job and talent growth data backs it up. But a city ranking high on talent and growth isn't the same as every role being easy to land, especially for someone early in their career. Here's what the actual data says about Toronto's tech market heading into 2026, and how to position yourself for it as a recent grad.

Toronto's standing in the data

CBRE's 2026 “Scoring Tech Talent” report, which ranks 75 of the largest tech markets across the US and Canada, has Toronto holding onto the No. 3 spot overall — behind only two US markets and ahead of every other city in North America. The report credits Toronto with adding 75,000 tech jobs between 2022 and 2025, more than any other market it tracks, and a workforce growth rate of 26.5% over that same period, one of the highest of any market in the ranking. Toronto isn't alone, either: eight Canadian cities land in CBRE's top 50, and six are in the top 15, which the report itself calls “impressive for a country our size.”

Where the growth is concentrated

A meaningful share of that growth is AI-driven. CBRE's report finds Toronto, Montreal, and Vancouver together hold 60% of Canada's entire AI talent pool, and the AI workforce across the US and Canada combined grew 45% to roughly 751,000 people as of mid-2026. That concentration matters for how you target your search: a title alone, like “software developer,” tells an employer less than specific, current tools do, and Toronto's growth is skewing toward candidates who can show real, hands-on experience with the platforms employers are actually hiring for.

The number worth knowing before you get discouraged: a growing market doesn't mean every opening is easy to land, especially at entry level. Indeed Hiring Lab data found Canadian tech job postings still sitting 19% below their February 2020 level, with mid-level and junior postings down a sharper 25% compared to five years earlier — a gap Indeed tied partly to high interest rates following the post-pandemic hiring frenzy and partly to overlap between what generative AI can now do and tasks listed in junior job descriptions. A high-level ranking like Toronto's No. 3 spot describes the health of the overall market, not how competitive any single entry-level posting is. Expect genuine competition for junior roles even in a market this strong.

Indeed Hiring Lab data, reported by The Logic, August 2025

What employers are actually paying for

Robert Half's Canada IT and tech salary guide points to machine learning, large language models, cloud platforms, cybersecurity, software architecture, enterprise systems, and data analytics as the skill clusters commanding the strongest demand right now, with named platforms including Azure, SAP S/4HANA, and Salesforce, and certifications like the AWS Certified Cloud Practitioner, CISSP, and Certified Ethical Hacker standing out to employers. The guide also found six in ten Canadian technology hiring managers are offering salaries above their originally planned range just to secure candidates with the right skills. The roles the guide highlights as commanding the biggest premiums — software architect, IT director, AI architect — are senior titles, but the underlying signal for someone earlier in their career is the same: naming specific, current tools and platforms on your resume, rather than broad category language like “cloud experience,” is what reads as credible to a Toronto employer evaluating a stack of similar-looking applications.

The broader Canadian picture

Toronto's growth isn't happening in isolation from the rest of the country, which matters if you're also weighing other Canadian cities. CBRE's report counts eight Canadian cities in its North American top 50 and six in the top 15, with Vancouver at No. 9, Waterloo Region at No. 10, and Montreal at No. 11 — a genuinely deep bench by the report's own framing, not just a single standout city. The AI workforce specifically is where the country's growth is concentrating fastest: tech companies accounted for 21% of US and Canadian office leasing in the first half of 2026, up from just 13% in 2023, which is a reasonable proxy for how much physical hiring and expansion is actually happening behind the talent-pool numbers rather than just being discussed.

Work authorization is part of the picture for many candidates

A significant number of IT job seekers in Toronto are international graduates working through a Post-Graduation Work Permit while they search, on top of everything else that goes into a job search. If that's your situation, we've covered how the PGWP actually works in a separate piece, since getting the permit mechanics right is something to sort out alongside the job search, not after it. Growx Tech IT doesn't provide immigration advice, but we do work with candidates navigating a PGWP timeline and a job search at the same time, where the resume, the search strategy, and the permit clock all need to line up.

Practical steps for a Toronto-focused search

Given a market that's genuinely growing at the top while staying tighter than the headlines suggest at entry level, a few adjustments matter more than they might in a less competitive city. Lead with the specific tools and platforms you've actually used, matching the concrete list above, rather than a generic “IT professional” framing that doesn't differentiate you from the next applicant in a deep, competitive pool. If your background touches AI, cloud, or cybersecurity even tangentially, make that explicit rather than implied, since that's exactly where Toronto's growth and Canada's broader AI talent concentration are pointing. And if you're earlier in your career, build in real runway for the search: a 25% drop in junior postings compared with five years ago means a longer time-to-offer is a market condition right now, not a reflection on your candidacy.

The bottom line

Toronto's tech market in late 2026 is genuinely a top-three North American talent hub by CBRE's own ranking, with real job growth to back it up — and it's also a market where entry-level postings specifically remain tighter than the headline ranking implies. Reading both numbers together, rather than just the flattering one, gives a far more accurate picture of what your search actually looks like.
