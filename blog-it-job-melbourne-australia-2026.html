How to Get an IT Job in Melbourne, Australia in 2026

November 16, 2026 · 7 min read · Growx Tech IT

Victoria's digital technology sector already employs more than 306,000 people and contributes over $36 billion a year to the state economy, roughly 30% of Australia's entire tech workforce, and the state government projects 19,300 more digital technology workers by 2028. Here's what the data actually shows about Melbourne's IT job market right now, and how to position yourself for it.

Melbourne's Tech Sector, by the Numbers

Victoria's digital technology sector contributes more than $36 billion a year to the state economy and employs over 306,000 people, which the Victorian government estimates at roughly 30% of Australia's entire tech workforce concentrated in a single state. That's a genuinely large base to be job hunting against, and it's growing: the Victorian Skills Plan projects 19,300 additional workers in digital technology occupations between 2025 and 2028, rising to 87,700 additional workers by 2035. Those are state-government planning figures, not marketing numbers, and they point to a labor market that's expected to keep adding IT roles well beyond this year.

New Investment Is Still Landing in Melbourne

That growth isn't only theoretical. In February 2026, Victoria's government announced that ITC Infotech, a technology services firm, would open a new Asia-Pacific headquarters and digital and AI engineering hub in Melbourne, creating what the state's Minister for Economic Growth and Jobs described as "hundreds of high-skilled jobs" (cited elsewhere as 150 specifically). The hub also includes an AI agritech component aimed at turning university research into practical farming tools, which reflects something distinctive about Melbourne's tech scene: a meaningful share of local demand sits at the intersection of software engineering and Victoria's other major industries, agriculture, health, and financial services, rather than purely in stand-alone tech companies.

AI Skills Are Showing Up Fastest in ICT Job Ads Specifically

On the demand side, SEEK's national employment data for September 2026 found that AI-related skill mentions in job ads rose 70.2% year over year, and that the ICT sector had the highest concentration of AI mentions of any industry, appearing in 14.9% of its job ads nationally, ahead of marketing and communications at 8.4%. That's a national figure rather than a Melbourne-specific one, but it's a useful signal for what to emphasize on a resume regardless of which Australian city you're applying in: naming the specific AI tools and platforms you've actually used is now a meaningfully stronger signal to recruiters than it was a year ago, simply because so many more ICT postings are screening for it.

Myth worth retiring: a single soft month of job ads doesn't mean Melbourne's tech market is shrinking. Australian job-ad volume is reported monthly and is naturally volatile; a dip or rise in any one month reflects the pace employers are posting at, not the size of the workforce they're running. Victoria's own government figures show a sector that has grown to over 306,000 workers and is forecast to keep adding jobs through 2028 and beyond, which is a structural, multi-year trend that a single month of hiring data doesn't override. The two kinds of numbers measure different things, and reading only one of them will give you a distorted sense of how competitive the market actually is right now.

What Roles and Skills Are Actually in Demand

Robert Half's 2026 Australia Technology & IT Salary Guide, covering the national market Melbourne sits within, lists cyber security specialists, data engineers, BI and data analysts, business analysts, systems engineers, cloud engineers, IT support staff, and AI/ML engineers as the roles employers are actively hiring for. On skills, the guide points to concrete, current demand for Azure and AWS specifically, DevOps practices including infrastructure-as-code and CI/CD, Power BI and the Microsoft Power Platform more broadly, and a growing cluster of AI-adjacent tools including Azure OpenAI, Semantic Kernel, and LangChain. For a Melbourne-based search, the practical implication is the same one that applies nationally: naming the specific platform or tool, not a category like "cloud experience," is what separates a resume that reads as current from one that reads as generic.

Work Rights Are Part of the Search for Many Candidates

A significant share of Melbourne's IT job seekers are international graduates or skilled migrants managing a visa timeline alongside the job search itself, most often the subclass 485 Temporary Graduate visa. If that's your situation, we've separately covered how the 485 visa's three streams, durations, and application window actually work, since getting that timeline right is a prerequisite to the search rather than something to sort out afterward. Growx Tech IT doesn't provide immigration advice, but we do work regularly with candidates managing exactly this kind of dual-track situation, where the resume, the interview timeline, and the visa clock all need to line up.

Practical Steps for Melbourne's Market Right Now

Given a workforce base this large and still growing, a few things matter more in Melbourne specifically than they might in a smaller market. Lead with whichever high-demand category your background actually sits in, cyber security, data engineering, or cloud, rather than a generic "IT professional" framing, since a market this size rewards specificity over breadth. If you've worked with any AI tooling at all, even in a limited capacity, name the specific tool rather than describing it generally, given how sharply AI-related screening has grown in ICT postings this year. And because Victoria's own hiring is partly driven by activity outside pure tech, agriculture-adjacent AI, health technology, financial services technology, it's worth tailoring your resume's framing toward the specific industry a role sits in rather than treating every Melbourne IT job as interchangeable.

The Bottom Line

Melbourne's IT job market in late 2026 sits on top of a genuinely large and still-growing base: a Victorian tech sector employing over 306,000 people, contributing more than $36 billion annually to the state economy, and forecast to add tens of thousands more roles by the end of the decade. New investment, like ITC Infotech's Asia-Pacific hub, keeps landing on top of that base, and AI-specific skills are becoming a sharper differentiator in how ICT roles get screened.
