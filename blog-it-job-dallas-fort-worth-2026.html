How to Get an IT Job in Dallas-Fort Worth in 2026

September 14, 2026 · 7 min read · Growx Tech IT

Dallas-Fort Worth just cracked the top 10 tech talent markets in North America. Here's what the data actually shows, and how to position yourself for it.

Dallas-Fort Worth has quietly become one of the strongest tech labor markets in the country, and most of the national headlines about layoffs and hiring freezes don't mention it. If you're job hunting for an IT role in DFW in 2026, the local numbers tell a very different story than the national news cycle.

DFW just cracked the top 10 tech talent markets in North America

CBRE's "Scoring Tech Talent 2026" report, a widely cited annual ranking of North American markets by tech workforce size and growth, placed Dallas-Fort Worth at No. 8 out of 50 markets, the region's first time in the top 10. Between 2022 and 2025, DFW added roughly 37,230 tech workers, the second-largest numeric gain of any market in North America behind Toronto, and a growth rate of 18.7% among large markets (those with 50,000 or more existing tech workers).

That growth isn't limited to one narrow niche. CBRE's data shows AI-specialized roles growing especially fast in the region, with DFW's specialized AI workforce up at least 45% year-over-year to roughly 29,840 workers, putting it sixth nationally for AI-specific talent concentration. CompTIA's own analysis separately ranked DFW fifth among U.S. metros for AI-related hiring and third nationally for overall tech job postings.

A common myth in a market like this: "the AI roles are just for data scientists with PhDs." In practice, CBRE's data shows AI-adjacent hiring concentrated heavily in financial services (20% of DFW's AI workforce, tied with New York) and professional/business services (25%), industries with a large presence in DFW that need people who can apply AI tools to existing workflows, not only build new models from scratch. Support, implementation, and analyst-level roles are a real part of that growth, not just research positions.

CBRE, "Scoring Tech Talent 2026" (via Dallas Innovates, Aug 25, 2026)

Why the market is growing even while national headlines talk about cuts

It's a fair question: if Big Tech keeps announcing layoffs nationally, how is a regional market adding tens of thousands of tech workers? Part of the answer is that DFW's tech growth is broad-based rather than concentrated in a handful of large tech-native employers making national headlines. The region's tech workforce spans financial services, telecom, healthcare, defense, and a large enterprise-technology base, companies that are hiring for tech roles even though they aren't "tech companies" in the way the national layoff coverage usually means. That mirrors a national pattern CompTIA has separately documented: technology occupations are growing across the broader economy even as staffing pulls back at some tech-sector companies specifically.

What this means if you're job hunting in DFW right now

A fast-growing regional market changes your job search math in a few concrete ways. Recruiters in a market with this much competition for talent are more likely to be actively sourcing on LinkedIn rather than waiting for inbound applications, which makes an up-to-date, keyword-complete profile more valuable here than in a slower market. It also means local employer research matters: a title like "business analyst" or "systems administrator" at a DFW bank or healthcare system may carry a heavier AI-tooling component than the same title did two years ago, so tailoring your resume to reflect any AI-adjacent tool experience you already have, even informally, is worth the extra ten minutes per application.

It's also worth being realistic about competition. A market growing this fast draws relocating candidates too, DFW's growth rate outpaces most of the country, so local employers are seeing applicants from higher-cost markets as well as homegrown talent. Standing out means being specific: a resume that names the actual tools, platforms, and measurable outcomes from your last role will do more in this market than one that lists generic responsibilities.

The bottom line for DFW job seekers

Dallas-Fort Worth isn't immune to the broader ups and downs of the tech labor market, but the regional data is genuinely stronger than the national headlines suggest. If you're searching for an IT job here, the opportunity is real, and comes with actual competition worth preparing for seriously.

Which skills and titles are actually in demand

CBRE's ranking methodology weighs both raw headcount growth and the concentration of specialized roles, which is why DFW's placement reflects more than one type of hiring. Beyond AI-specific titles (machine learning engineer, AI implementation specialist, data scientist), the region's underlying growth is showing up in cloud infrastructure, cybersecurity, and enterprise systems roles tied to the financial services and telecom employers headquartered or heavily staffed in the metro. Nationally, CompTIA's State of the Tech Workforce 2026 report projects cybersecurity analyst and engineer roles growing 346% and data scientist and analyst roles growing 420% over the next decade, both well ahead of the 188% projected for software developers and engineers, a useful signal for DFW job seekers deciding where to invest certification or upskilling time.

Texas as a whole led all states for net new tech jobs in CompTIA's 2026 data, with a projected addition of 32,238 positions, well ahead of California's 16,949 and Florida's 14,453. Dallas ranked among the top individual metro areas nationally for projected new tech jobs. That state-level tailwind is part of why DFW's growth looks less like a temporary spike and more like a sustained shift in where tech hiring is concentrated.

Practical steps for landing a role in this market

Given the pace of growth, a few things matter more here than in a flatter market: applying quickly once a posting goes live (fast-growing markets see postings filled faster), leading with a resume summary that names the specific industry vertical you're targeting (financial services, healthcare, telecom, or enterprise tech) rather than a generic "IT professional" framing, and being ready to speak concretely about any AI tools you've used even in a support or implementation capacity, since a large share of DFW's growth sits in applying AI within existing industries rather than building new AI products from scratch.
