How to Get an IT Job in Sydney, Australia in 2026

November 2, 2026 · 7 min read · Growx Tech IT

Australia's tech workforce has grown toward 1 million people and software engineering roles are up 7% year over year nationally, even as month-to-month ICT job ads have actually softened. Here's what that mixed picture means if you're job hunting for an IT role in Sydney right now, and how to position yourself.

Sydney's tech sector gets talked about in two very different registers depending on which number you look at. The long-run story is one of real, structural growth. The month-to-month story, at least recently, has been softer. Both are true at the same time, and understanding the gap between them matters if you're actually applying for IT roles in Sydney right now rather than just reading headlines about the market.

The long-run number: a tech workforce approaching 1 million

The Tech Council of Australia's annual jobs report, published in August 2026, put the national tech workforce at roughly 977,000 people as of November 2025, split between about 424,000 people working directly in tech roles and another 553,000 working in technical roles embedded in other industries. Within that, software engineering roles specifically grew 7% year over year, and the report also flagged a notable regional trend: tech jobs outside Australia's major cities grew 12% over five years, more than double the growth rate of regional employment overall. That's the structural backdrop: a tech workforce that has grown substantially and kept growing even as hiring conditions elsewhere have tightened.

The monthly picture: ICT job ads have actually cooled

Set against that, SEEK's employment report for September 2026 shows a softer near-term trend specifically in technology hiring. Information and Communication Technology job ads fell 3.7% nationally month over month, one of the larger industry declines that month, with Victoria's ICT ads down an even sharper 6.8%. New South Wales overall, Sydney's state, actually posted a third consecutive monthly rise in total job ads, up 0.5%, but that growth was led by education and training, manufacturing and logistics, and trades and services, not technology specifically.

Myth worth retiring: a soft month of ICT job ads doesn't mean the tech workforce is shrinking. Monthly job-ad data is volatile by nature and reflects hiring pace, not headcount: a 3.7% monthly dip in ad volume is consistent with employers slowing the rate of new postings while still running a workforce that grew structurally over the past year. The Tech Council's year-over-year figures and SEEK's month-over-month ad counts are measuring different things, and treating either one alone as the full picture will give you a distorted read on how competitive the market actually is right now.

What roles and skills are actually in demand

Robert Half's 2026 Australia Technology & IT Salary Guide lists the roles employers are actively hiring for as cyber-security specialists, data engineers, BI and data analysts, business analysts, systems engineers, cloud engineers, IT support, and AI/ML engineers. On the skills side, the guide points to strong, specific demand for cloud platforms (Azure and AWS), DevOps practices including infrastructure-as-code and CI/CD, Power BI and the broader Microsoft Power Platform, and a growing cluster of AI-adjacent tools including Azure OpenAI, Semantic Kernel, and LangChain. The guide also notes that salary growth for several roles, including business analysts and project managers, has plateaued after a few more volatile years, and that AI adoption inside Australian employers is still in its early stages rather than fully mature. For a resume, that mix is a useful signal: naming specific, current tools rather than broad category terms like "cloud experience" or "AI skills" is what separates a resume that reads as current from one that reads as dated.

Why Sydney specifically

Sydney isn't just Australia's largest city; it's also home to the Australian headquarters of most major banks, insurers, and a large share of the multinational technology and consulting firms operating in the country, which makes financial-services technology, enterprise software, and consulting-adjacent IT roles a particularly deep part of the local market compared with smaller Australian cities. That concentration cuts both ways for a job seeker: there's more volume of roles to apply to, but also more competition from a larger pool of candidates drawn to the same market, which is part of why resume specificity and applying early matter more here than in a thinner market.

Work rights are part of the equation for many candidates

A meaningful share of Sydney's IT job seekers are international graduates or skilled migrants navigating a visa alongside the job search itself, most commonly the subclass 485 Temporary Graduate visa. If that applies to you, we've covered how the 485 visa's three streams and timelines work in a separate piece, since getting the visa mechanics right is a prerequisite to the job search, not an afterthought. Growx Tech IT doesn't provide immigration advice, but we do work with candidates on exactly this kind of dual-track situation, where the resume, the timeline, and the visa status all have to line up.

Practical steps for the Sydney market right now

Given the gap between strong structural growth and a softer monthly ad pace, a few adjustments matter more than usual. Apply to roles promptly once they post rather than waiting, since a slower posting pace tends to mean more applicants per listing, not fewer. Name specific tools and platforms on your resume and LinkedIn profile, matching the concrete list above, rather than generic category language that doesn't differentiate you in a market where employers are being more selective about what they post for. If your background sits in one of the higher-demand categories, cyber security, data engineering, or cloud, lead with that specifically rather than burying it under a generic "IT professional" title. And if you're newer to the market or coming off a 485 visa timeline, build in extra runway for your search, since a softer monthly hiring pace generally means a longer time-to-offer even in a structurally growing field.

The bottom line

Sydney's IT job market in late 2026 is genuinely two things at once: a workforce that has grown toward 1 million nationally with real structural momentum behind it, and a near-term hiring pace that cooled in September specifically in technology job ads. Reading both numbers, instead of just one, gives a far more accurate picture of how competitive your search actually is.
